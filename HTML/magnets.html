How do magnets work?

Animation

Magnets work through an invisible force called a magnetic field, which is created by the movement and alignment of tiny particles called electrons.


Every electron spins around the nucleus of an atom, acting like a microscopic magnet with its own mini magnetic field. In most materials, these microscopic fields point in random directions and cancel each other out. However, in ferromagnetic materials like iron, nickel, and cobalt, these atomic fields naturally line up into clusters called magnetic domains.


When an external force or another magnet causes all these domains to point in the exact same direction, their individual strengths combine to create a macroscopic magnet. This uniform alignment creates a distinct north and south pole, resulting in the classic rule of magnetism where opposite poles attract and identical poles repel each other.





Types of magnets



Animation

1.Permanent Magnets



Permanent magnets keep their magnetic field all the time without needing power. Common materials include:

Neodymium: The strongest type of permanent magnet, made from an alloy of neodymium, iron, and boron. Used in hard drives and headphones.

Samarium Cobalt: A strong rare-earth magnet that handles high heat very well. Used in aerospace and military tech.

Alnico: Made from aluminum, nickel, and cobalt. Offers good heat resistance and is used in electric motors and sensors.

Ferrite (Ceramic): Inexpensive and brittle, made of iron oxide and barium or strontium. Used in refrigerator magnets and speakers.



2.Temporary Magnets

Temporary magnets act like magnets only when they are inside a strong magnetic field. When the field goes away, they lose their magnetism.

Examples: Iron nails, paperclips, and soft iron items.

Use: Picking up metal scraps or conducting magnetic force.



3.Electromagnets

Electromagnets use electricity to create a magnetic field. A wire coil is wrapped around a metal core (like iron). When an electric current flows through the wire, it turns into a magnet. When the power turns off, the magnetism stops.

Examples: Industrial scrap yard cranes, MRI machines, and electric bells.
